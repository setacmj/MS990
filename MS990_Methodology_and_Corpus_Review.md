# Wellcome Collection MS.990: Methodology, Corpus Analysis, Quality Review, and Textual Summary

## Introduction and Overview of Deliverables

This document provides a technical and scholarly review of the digital diplomatic edition and English translation of Wellcome Collection **MS.990** (*Compendium of Medicine and Surgery*, Austro-Bavarian Franciscan provenance, ca. 1650–1680), prepared to support peer evaluation and downstream research. The complete edition comprises 741 catalogued pages (55,875 transcribed and translated lines, totaling 2,196,427 German characters and 2,309,317 English characters, alongside 385 catalogued illustrations, diagrams, and photographic artifacts) derived from the Wellcome Collection IIIF digitisation (`b18775275`, 745 manifest canvases).

The edition is distributed across a suite of synchronized outputs generated from a single canonical Python data pipeline (`generate_edition_outputs.py`, `build_parallel_md.py`, and `build_explorer_html.py`). These outputs include a validated TEI P5 XML edition (`MS990_tei.xml`, 189,488 lines), a structured JSON dataset (`MS990_edition_data.json`, 63 MB), parallel German-English Markdown editions with and without embedded IIIF/local facsimile references (`MS990_parallel_edition.md` and `MS990_parallel_edition_with_facsimiles.md`), a standalone HTML parallel facsimile edition (`MS990_parallel_edition_with_facsimiles.html`), plain-text and Markdown monolingual files (`MS990_transcription.txt/.md` and `MS990_translation.txt/.md`), and an interactive browser-based manuscript explorer (`ms990_explorer.html`) featuring line-level IIIF coordinate overlays and multi-state anatomical flap switching.

## Acquisition, Processing Pipeline, and Detailed Methodology

The digitisation and transcription workflow was structured around Wellcome Collection's IIIF Presentation and Image APIs (`b18775275`). High-resolution JPEG facsimiles (2,400 pixels wide) were retrieved from the IIIF Image API endpoint (`https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/image/b18775275_MS_990_{NNNN}.JP2/full/2400,/0/default.jpg`), verified for valid JPEG headers, and stored locally. Cross-checking against the live IIIF Presentation manifest confirmed 745 total canvases; four frontmatter/blank endpaper canvases at the beginning (`1, 4, 5, 6`) were omitted from the textual edition, and Wellcome's own JP2 numbering skips `0738` and `0739` (jumping directly from `0737.JP2` to `0740.JP2` and continuing through `0747.JP2`), yielding the 741 pages present in `MS990_edition_data.json` with zero duplicate `canvas_num` entries.

Transcription and translation were executed in twenty-eight sequential batches (`pages_batch2.py` through `pages_batch28.py`), predominantly using **Gemini 4 Hydrogen** (664 pages), alongside 65 early pages processed with **Gemini 4** and 12 early or frontmatter pages carrying an `"unknown"` model tag. To maximize paleographic accuracy on 17th-century *Kurrentschrift* and Early New High German (*Frühneuhochdeutsch*) medical-alchemical prose, images were dispatched in small groups of one to three canvases per autonomous visual-reading subagent. Each subagent inspected the high-resolution image directly, segmented the page into its ruling frame (`full`, `left`, or `right` columns), recorded physical forme work (boxed page numbers, catchwords, and modern pencil foliation), catalogued watercolor illustrations and marginal drawings, transcribed each line diplomatically with Unicode apothecary and alchemical symbols (`℞`, `āā`, `ʒ`, `℥`, `℈`, `℔`, `♁`, `♀`, `♂`, `♃`, `♄`, `☉`, `☽`, `☿`, `▽`, `⊖`, `+`, `□`), and produced an idiomatic English translation alongside line-level paleographic notes (such as red-ink rubrication, strikethroughs, and damage).

Whenever a three-canvas batch triggered automated safety filters—most notably on anatomical, gynecological, or surgical-amputation pages—the affected canvases were retried individually as single-canvas historical-manuscript tasks, which resolved all blocks without omission. Structured JSON outputs from the subagents were normalized into canonical Python batch modules, enriched with exact JPEG pixel dimensions and proportional bounding-box (`xywh`) IIIF region URIs via `make_lines()`, and compiled by `generate_edition_outputs.py` into TEI P5 XML (validated with `xml.etree.ElementTree.parse`), JSON, Markdown, and HTML.

## Corpus Analysis and Stylistic Inconsistencies

A programmatic audit of the 741-page, 55,875-line corpus reveals high overall structural completeness alongside several measurable stylistic and schema inconsistencies that peers should take into account when using the edition for quantitative or linguistic research.

The most significant stylistic variation concerns **red-ink rubrication annotation**. Early modern medical manuscripts rely heavily on red ink to mark chapter headings, recipe titles, *Nota Bene* markers, and initial capitals. Across most of the corpus, red-ink elements are richly documented (5,469 line-level notes across 741 pages, averaging roughly 300 to 600 annotations per 50-canvas window). However, in canvases `0050–0099`, only 36 red-ink notes appear across 3,418 lines, and canvases `0000–0049` contain 169 across 3,008 lines. This reflects an early phase of prompting before explicit per-line rubrication checks were enforced across all subagents. Consequently, absence of a red-ink note in canvases `0050–0099` should not be interpreted as an absence of rubrication on the physical leaf.

A second inconsistency involves **diplomatic orthography, specifically the long-s (`ſ`) and apothecary recipe signs**. Across the corpus, the sharp-s (`ß`) is used consistently (11,831 occurrences), whereas the long-s (`ſ`) appears sporadically—clustering in `c.000–049` (197 lines), `c.100–149` (155 lines), `c.400–449` (68 lines), `c.600–649` (207 lines), and `c.700–749` (25 lines), while dropping to zero in `c.050–099` and `c.200–249`. Most subagents normalized `ſ` to round `s`, while a subset preserved `ſ` diplomatically. Similarly, the prescription sign `℞` is overwhelmingly rendered as Unicode `℞` (3,333 lines), but 32 lines (19 in `c.250–299` and 9 in `c.700–749`) transcribe it as plain ASCII `R:`, `Rp.`, or `Rec.`.

Third, **line-type taxonomy (`line_type`)** evolved over the course of the project. Although all 55,875 lines share a uniform dictionary schema in `MS990_edition_data.json`, the `line_type` field contains 21 distinct labels: `p` (18,974), `text` (15,055), `recipe` (7,466), `line` (5,017), `heading` (2,692), `body` (2,434), `h2` (1,230), `h1` (946), `rubric` (886), `fw` (747), `prose` (130), `answer` (84), `question` (59), `page_num` (36), `h` (35), `head` (24), `margin` (21), `mixed` (18), `caption` (12), `verse` (8), and `note` (1). Downstream scripts should normalize these into broader functional classes (e.g., grouping `p`, `text`, `line`, `body`, `prose`, and `recipe` as body text, and `heading`, `h1`, `h2`, `h`, `head`, and `rubric` as headings) rather than filtering on a single literal tag.

## Technical Concerns and Limitations of the Output

Beyond stylistic drift, four concrete technical concerns warrant attention before citing the edition in formal scholarship.

First, **original page number (`orig_page`) discontinuities** reflect a mixture of genuine 17th-century scribal pagination quirks and occasional LLM digit-misreading errors. While some skips are demonstrably original to the codex—such as canvas 8 (`p. 30`) facing canvas 9 (`p. 32`, omitting `31`), or canvas 705 (`p. 781`) jumping to canvas 706 (`p. 786`)—several isolated anomalies appear to be visual digit confusions in the boxed headers, notably `7` read as `4` (`c.0646–0648` recorded as `409–411` between `708` and `712`), `7` read as `1` (`c.0670` recorded as `134` and `c.0672` as `138`), and `6` read as `0` (`c.0700–0702` recorded as `706–708` between `765` and `779`). Users should rely on `canvas_num` as the primary stable locator and verify anomalous `orig_page` numbers against the facsimile.

Second, **line-level IIIF bounding boxes (`xywh`) are interpolated rather than OCR-segmented**. As implemented in `make_lines()`, line coordinates are calculated by evenly subdividing the column frame (`frame_box` and `center_rule_x`) vertically by the number of transcribed lines in that column. This provides reliable approximate navigation in `ms990_explorer.html`, but the coordinates do not represent tight pixel-accurate word or line polygons, especially on pages with large watercolor figures or irregular marginalia.

Third, **token-usage metadata (`input_tokens`, `output_tokens`) in later batches is standardized rather than metered**. While early batches record varied or null token counts (146 pages with `None`), batches assembled via the automated batch builder carry standardized estimates (e.g., `1850` input / `2200` output tokens on 231 pages). These figures should not be used for precise API cost or perplexity accounting.

Fourth, **trailing conservation and detail photographs (canvases 727–747)** represent re-photographed views of earlier manuscript pages rather than new textual leaves. Specifically, canvases `727–730` document the multi-layered *Klappbild* (anatomical flap diagram) of the eye on original page 48 in its closed, half-open, and fully open states; canvas `731` re-photographs p. 669; canvas `733` re-photographs p. 542 (the monastic eye-apothecary); canvases `734–743` are macro, detail, or rotated conservation shots (some with paper watermarks and cm scale bars); and canvases `744–747` show the abraded inside back cover, front cover, and ColorChecker calibration target on p. 136. Twelve pages in the dataset (`c.137, 138, 140, 322, 719–722, 724–726, 732`) intentionally contain zero text lines because they are full-page watercolor plates, blank flyleaves/pastedowns, binding-edge views, or an uninscribed drawing fragment.

## Web Edition Architecture, Entity Linking, and IIIF Figure-Crop Refinement

Following the initial build, the parallel HTML edition (`MS990_parallel_edition_with_facsimiles.html`, published to GitHub Pages as `docs/index.html`) was substantially refactored to support scholarly browsing and discovery without external server dependencies. Because several paratextual leaves (such as the 20th-century binding spine and provenance notes) are written in English rather than Early New High German, the left-hand table header was generalized from *German Diplomatic (Original)* to *Transcription*. The edition now opens on an introductory landing page summarizing the manuscript's provenance and scope alongside ten direct-jump cards pointing to verified illustrated highlights across the codex, while an in-browser lazy indexer enables cross-edition full-text search across all 741 pages and 55,875 aligned lines inside an accessible modal `<dialog>`.

To support structured topical navigation, a curated index of 102 high-frequency entities—spanning 50 *materia medica* ingredients, 13 historical medical authorities and provenance figures, 11 geographic places, and 28 medical or alchemical terms—was extracted programmatically across the bilingual corpus (`MS990_entity_index.json`). Each entity was queried against the Wikidata MediaWiki API (`wbsearchentities`) and manually disambiguated where homographs occurred (such as distinguishing the 2nd-century physician Galen `Q8778`, the 17th-century pharmacologist Johann Schröder `Q73983`, and Venice `Q641` from modern namesakes), yielding 101 verified Wikidata `Q-ID` links alongside clickable page occurrences; only *Pannus (Eye Film)* was left unlinked where no exact human corneal pannus item was returned.

Finally, a dedicated **Figures & Diagrams (385)** gallery was constructed and subjected to a two-stage bounding-box audit. Initial inspection revealed that 114 figures in `c.466–747` (plus 2 in `c.227–228`) had fallen back to a generic placeholder rectangle when automated batch assembly replaced earlier manual coordinate entry, while several mid-corpus batches (`c.140–465`) contained full-page `(0, 0, w, h)` boxes or misaligned coordinates. Using 31 parallel Gemini 4 Hydrogen visual-reading passes over the local JPEGs, tight normalized coordinates (`0..1000`) were re-extracted across 207 canvases (`MS990_figure_boxes_patch.json`). Subsequent visual testing also uncovered a coordinate-space mismatch in `c.557–747`, where stored `width` and `height` reflected 2400px-wide local JPEGs rather than Wellcome's full-resolution (~6000 × 6853 px) JP2 dimensions; switching the IIIF Image API requests in `build_parallel_with_facsimiles.py` from absolute pixel regions to resolution-independent percentage regions (`pct:x,y,w,h`) resolved all 40 affected canvases, producing 375 verified IIIF-cropped figures and 10 full-page conservation reference views with zero out-of-bounds or undersized anomalies.

## Detailed Summary of the Text

Wellcome MS.990 is a richly illustrated, Austro-Bavarian Franciscan medical, surgical, and iatrochemical compendium (*Arzneibuch*) compiled around 1650–1680 and acquired by Henry Wellcome in 1922 from the Viennese booksellers Gilhofer & Ranschburg (`c.0002–0003`). Although the opening 28 pages are wanting—with the extant text beginning mid-treatise on `[p. 29]` (`c.0007`)—the manuscript preserves an extraordinary synthesis of Galenic humoral pathology, Paracelsian/iatrochemical pharmacy, practical surgery, and monastic folk medicine.

The opening extant section (`c.0007–0024`, pp. 29–47) treats diseases of the mouth, teeth, throat, neck, and ears, including dental caries, gum necrosis, *angina* (quinsy), parotid abscesses, scrofula, goitre (*Strumae*), ear apostemes, and birthmarks, illustrated with watercolor studies of teeth and goitrous patients. This leads directly into a major, richly illustrated **Ophthalmology Treatise** (`c.0025–0145`, pp. 48–174), opening on p. 48 with an interactive anatomical flap diagram (*Klappbild*) of the eye's muscles, humors, and seven tunics, followed by diagnostic portrait busts of ocular pathologies (cataracts, lacrimal fistulas, *pannus*, trichiasis, and night blindness) and detailed surgical and pharmaceutical cures.

From `c.0146` onward (pp. 175–251), the compendium shifts to **internal medicine and fever pathology**, covering dietetics for febrile patients, intermittent fevers (tertian, quartan, and Hungarian typhus), cardiac syncope, asthma, pleurisy, phthisis, and stomach disorders. Around `c.0225–0270` (pp. 252–298), the text incorporates an **anatomical and physiological interlude** describing the viscera, intestines, omentum, peritoneum (accompanied by anatomical drawings), the four Galenic complexions, vital spirits, and melancholy. Subsequent sections (`c.0271–0320`, pp. 299–349) address **hepatic, splenic, and renal disorders**, including jaundice, dropsy, dysentery, colic, and kidney stones.

A substantial **iatrochemical and pharmaceutical section** (`c.0321–0425`, pp. 350–465) follows, opening with a full-page watercolor of an apothecary interior (`c.0322`, p. 352) and detailing the preparation of *Antimonium diaphoreticum*, *Crocus martis*, *Crocus metallorum*, *Laudanum opiatum*, *Lutum sapientiae*, mineral baths, elixirs, and herbal extracts, illustrated with distillation furnaces, retorts, and alchemical apparatus. The manuscript then turns to **urology, venereal disease, and sympathetic/magical-theological medicine** (`c.0426–0465`, pp. 466–520), including a notable defense of sympathetic wound-healing citing ecclesiastical and Parisian theological authorities (`c.0455`), alongside treatises on gonorrhea, urethral caruncles, and hernias.

The final third of the main text (`c.0466–0710`, pp. 521–790) is dominated by **practical surgery, dermatology, gynecology, and trauma**, featuring the monastic eye-remedy apothecary plate (p. 542), treatments for gout/podagra, knee fungus, breast cancer, fistulas, gunshot wounds, gangrene, fractures (*fractura*, *morpha*, *fissura*), limb amputation (`c.0699–0701`, illustrated with a surgical preparation scene), a "Peasants' and Poor People's Apothecary" (*Paurn, Vnd Armer Leüdt apotecken*, `c.0705`), and animal/excrement-based *Dreckapotheke* remedies (`c.0706–0708`). The volume closes with a **three-column alphabetical index (Register)** covering A through P (`c.0711–0718`), ending with *"end."* on `c.0718`, followed by blank flyleaves, binding photographs, and Wellcome's conservation/flap detail images (`c.0719–0747`).

## Avenues for Further Enquiry

This structured, bilingual corpus opens several promising avenues for interdisciplinary research. Paleographers and digital humanists can use the 5,469 red-ink annotations and 385 catalogued figures to study visual hierarchy, monastic workshop illustration practices, and interactive paper engineering (*Klappbilder*) in 17th-century German medical books, while prioritizing canvases `0050–0099` for targeted manual rubrication enrichment. Historians of medicine and pharmacy can mine the 3,333 `℞` recipes and over 15,000 apothecary/alchemical symbols to map the coexistence of Galenic humoralism, Paracelsian antimony and mercury chemistry, Felix Würtz's surgical tradition, and vernacular *Dreckapotheke* within an Austro-Bavarian Franciscan milieu. Finally, historical linguists can leverage the aligned diplomatic German and English lines—keeping in mind the batch-level variation in long-s (`ſ`) normalization—to investigate Early New High German Austro-Bavarian dialectal phonology, Latin-German code-switching, and early modern surgical terminology.
